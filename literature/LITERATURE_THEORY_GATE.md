## 1. Object mismatch (decisive for novelty claims)
- DDF: sets D ⊆ K^n, K a **field**; theories of fields; efd is geometric (transcendence degree, fibre dimension of varieties); results for ACF/RCF/Q_p/PAC/Hilbertian/global fields.
- mpup (Spec.lean, frozen): A ⊆ ℕ^n, unknowns in ℕ or ℤ, coefficients in ℤ, "degree" = MvPolynomial.totalDegree over all variables (parameters included) — the Matiyasevich / BDHMS 2025 universal-pair setting.
- Consequently: DDF's existential rank of a *set* is the analogue of ν (witness count) **without** the degree filter and **over fields**. There is no degree-filtered rank in DDF Def 1.3 or in the parts read [V]. The degree filter is what BDHMS §6.2 introduces (universal pair (ν, δ)).

## 2. What the counting principle is (folklore) and what is ours
- Folklore: an injective polynomial map ℕ^n → ℤ^m of degrees ≤ e_j forces n ≤ Σ e_j (box counting). Sources in LITERATURE_NOTE_20260826.md (Cantor, Fueter–Pólya, Lew–Rosenberg, Nathanson 2016). Cite as folklore; do not claim.
- Ours (r03 frozen, [P] std-3): the reduction §6.2-universality ⇒ graph coder ⇒ specialization injective, and the kernel-checked bound. r04 adds: exact S(d,q) (needs Skolem lower bound), weighted G ≤ C(d+r+1,r+2), sandwich U−1 ≤ G ≤ S, G(r,1)=1, G(0,d)=d (semantic root bound), tree-coding lower bound.

Lemma 3.2 (§3.2, verbatim): "Suppose that a given binary quadratic Diophantine equation system (3.1)-(3.6) is either definite or degenerate. Its proof: definite (D < 0) — every integer solution of y₁² − Dy₂² = g has max(|y₁|,|y₂|) ≤ √|g| (Case 1); degenerate D = h² — factorisation (y₁+hy₂)(y₁−hy₂) = g (Case 1, (3.12)); Case 3 a = c = 0 — factorisation 2(bx₁+e)(bx₂+d) = 2de − bf; Case 4 D = 0 — quadratic part = m(αx₁+βx₂)², substitute z = αx₁+βx₂, then either a univariate quadratic in z or x₁, x₂ rational functions of z with a congruence class of z (mod 2(eα−dβ)Γ) deciding integrality, and sign changes located at |z| < 6||F||². Indefinite case = §3.3 ff., reduced to y₁² − Dy₂² = g with side conditions and handled by Gauss's theory of forms + Shanks's infrastructure (certificates, not our concern).

**Crosswalk to our six classes.** Lagarias' three classes are by D = b² − ac = Δ/4 in our notation (his 2b = our B): definite = D1; degenerate (perfect square, including 0) = our D2 ∪ D3 (Δ = 0) ∪ D4 ∪ D6 (Δ = δ² > 0), with our C = 0 sub-splits corresponding to his Cases 2–4; indefinite = D7. Our D7 mechanism (a unit power ≡ (1,0) mod 8Δ|C| preserving integrality and a congruence class, then a strictly larger c ≥ 0) is a congruence-plus-nonnegativity side-condition argument of exactly the shape his admissibility framework is built for.

**What is his, what is ours (novelty gate).** His: the definite/degenerate/indefinite trichotomy by the determinant and the small-solution bound for the definite and degenerate classes (Lemma 3.2 bounds the *least* admissible solution; the definite proof in fact bounds *all* solutions, and the degenerate factorisation bounds all solutions with g ≠ 0). Ours: (i) the **projection dichotomy** — for fixed quadratic part (A,B,C) a single K such that, for every linear part (l,m,q), the projected set S ⊂ ℕ is infinite or bounded by K((1+|l|+|m|)² + |q|), uniform in (l,m,q) and *quadratic in the linear coefficients* (his bounds are polynomial in the whole ||F||, not tracked separately in the linear part — that separation is what the box-counting step needs); (ii) the indefinite "infinite whenever a solution with X > 0 exists" branch as a kernel-checked statement; (iii) the application to an exact graph capacity G_ℤ(1,2) = 2 (`graphCapacityZ_one_two`).
